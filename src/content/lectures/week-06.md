Incubation — stepping away from a problem and having a solution arrive
unbidden — is one of the most consistently reported and least understood
effects in the study of thinking. What's fairly well established is *when* it
tends to strike: during low-demand, semi-automatic activity, rarely during
focused effort and rarely during full rest either. What's much less settled
is what the mind was doing with the problem in the interval nobody was
watching it.

## Outline

- incubation and the shower-thought effect: what the research actually shows
- why an intuition arriving isn't the same event as the reasoning that later
