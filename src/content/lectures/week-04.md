When no external task is occupying attention, the brain doesn't go quiet — a
specific, well-studied network of regions (the "default mode network")
becomes more active, not less. Mind-wandering during boredom isn't the
absence of mental activity; it's a switch to a different kind of activity,
one usually pointed at autobiographical memory, imagined futures, and other
people's mental states rather than the present task.
