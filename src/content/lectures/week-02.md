Most philosophy of mind is written as though consciousness is a private
event happening inside one skull at a time. Uncontrollable laughter is a
useful early counterexample: it spreads, it escalates by feeding on itself
across a room, and it is measurably harder to summon alone than in company.
If some conscious states are partly constituted by other people's presence,
the private, one-mind-at-a-time picture is already too small.
