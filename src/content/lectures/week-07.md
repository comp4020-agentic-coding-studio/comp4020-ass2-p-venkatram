Smell is unusual among the senses in its wiring: olfactory signals reach the
brain's memory and emotion centres by a more direct route than sight or
sound, which pass through more processing stops first. That anatomical
shortcut is one candidate explanation for the "Proust effect" — a smell
delivering a whole, specific, unrequested memory faster and more completely
than deliberate recall usually manages.

## Outline

- the olfactory system's comparatively direct route to memory and emotion,
  and why that's one plausible mechanism behind involuntary nostalgia
- why the memories a scent surfaces are so often ones you couldn't have
  retrieved on request
- opening unit 3: three weeks of experience that only shows up across time,
  or between people, rather than in a single isolated mind
