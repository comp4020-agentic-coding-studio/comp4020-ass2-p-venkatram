Every other course you've taken about the mind hands you a description and
calls it understanding. This one starts from the opposite premise: a perfect
third-person account of what happens in a nervous system during pain still
leaves out the one thing pain actually is to the person having it. That gap —
between the complete physical story and the felt fact of experience — is
usually called the explanatory gap, and it's the reason this course is a
practicum rather than a seminar.

## Outline

- the hard problem of consciousness, stated plainly: not "how does the brain
  work" but "why is there something it is like to be a brain doing that"
- Nagel's bat, and why imagining sonar tells you nothing about having it
- the shape of the semester: four units, twelve inductions, one open question
- what "doing the practicum" actually requires of you each week, and why
  skipping straight to the write-up defeats the point
