An empty room and a silent room with a trusted friend in it produce very
different experiences from an identical absence of sound. The difference
can't be explained by anything happening acoustically — it has to be
explained by something about co-presence itself: a felt sense of another
mind, unoccupied by performance, sharing the same unstructured moment. That's
a much harder thing for a private, one-mind theory of consciousness to
account for than an ordinary shared task is.

## Outline

- extended and social theories of mind, briefly: is some conscious experience
  partly constituted by another person's presence, not just caused by it
- why an "awkward" silence and a "comfortable" one can have identical acoustic
  content and completely different phenomenology
- carrying week 2's contagious laughter and this week's silence into the same
  question: how much of consciousness actually happens between people
