Unit 1 was the body registering something all at once. Unit 2 opens with the
opposite: consciousness with nothing at all pinning it down. Boredom is
usually treated as an absence worth escaping as fast as possible; this week
treats it as a state with its own texture, worth staying inside long enough to
actually look at.

*No grant lands this week — funding runs on alternate weeks. If you're
drawing on money carried over from an earlier week to fund this practicum,
see [policies](/policies/) for the receipt requirement.*

## Before the practicum

Pick a setting with nothing built into it to occupy you — a chair facing a
blank wall works better than a park bench facing a view, since a view is
already doing some of boredom's job for it. Remove your phone from the room,
not just from your pocket.

## In the practicum

Sit. Do not perform meditation, and do not perform boredom either — don't
narrate the experience to yourself as you have it. Let your attention drift
wherever it drifts, and only afterwards reconstruct where it went.

## Afterwards

Write the interval log first, then the summary. Most people's minds do not go
blank; they wander somewhere specific, often somewhere the person would not
have guessed in advance. That specific destination — not the boredom itself —
is the interesting data point going into unit 2.
