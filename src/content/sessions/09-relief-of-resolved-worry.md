This week only works if you bring a real stake into it — a manufactured worry
produces a manufactured relief. Unlike every experience so far, this one
can't be scheduled: you can set the conditions for noticing it, but you can't
force the resolution itself to arrive on a timetable, which is itself part of
what the week is teaching.

## Before the practicum

Name an actual worry you are currently carrying — something genuinely
unresolved, not a hypothetical. Write down, before anything happens, exactly
what "resolved" would look like for this particular worry.

## In the practicum

Go about the week as normal, but stay alert for the resolution if and when it
comes. Most people report a distinct bodily component to relief — a lightness
in the chest, an exhale, a drop in the shoulders — that arrives slightly
before the thought "I don't need to worry about that anymore." Try to catch
which comes first.

## Afterwards

Write the note either way. A worry that resolves cleanly during the week
gives you the sensation this week is named for; a worry that doesn't gives
you something just as useful for unit 3's report — what it is to sit with an
open account, unresolved, and still have to write about it honestly.
