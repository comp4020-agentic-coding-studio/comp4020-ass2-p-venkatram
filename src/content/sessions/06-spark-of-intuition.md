Unit 2 closes on the state most people already recognise but rarely examine:
the answer that shows up before the steps that supposedly produced it. This
week asks you to engineer the conditions for that arrival on purpose, rather
than waiting for it to happen to you.

## Before the practicum

Choose a real problem — a piece of writing that isn't working, a design
decision, a puzzle, a technical bug — that you have genuinely tried to solve
and gotten stuck on. It has to be a problem you care about the answer to, not
an artificial one built for this exercise.

## In the practicum

Work the problem directly for a fixed period, then deliberately stop and do
something unrelated and non-verbal — a walk, a shower, tidying a room. Do not
try to force the answer during the break; the break is the method.

## Afterwards

If an answer or a usable fragment of one arrives, write down the exact
circumstance — what you were doing, what triggered the noticing — before you
do any work checking whether it's actually correct. Keep that arrival note
separate from your later verification. The two are different kinds of mental
event wearing the same name, and unit 3 depends on you being able to tell them
apart.
