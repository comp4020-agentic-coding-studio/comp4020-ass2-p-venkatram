This closes unit 1 by asking what changes when the sensation is earned rather
than merely endured. Physical tiredness on its own is not interesting to this
course; the tiredness that arrives with a finished, visible thing attached to
it is a different experience wearing the same bodily signs, and this week is
about catching the difference.

## Before the practicum

Choose labor with an outcome you can point to afterwards, not a workout. A run
that leaves nothing behind but a number on a watch is the wrong choice this
week — save it for the comparison.

## In the practicum

Work until the ache is real, not performative. Somewhere near the end, notice
the shift: the same muscular sensation that would read as unwelcome fatigue in
another context starts reading as confirmation. That's the thing to catch,
mid-shift, not in retrospect.

## Afterwards

Do the comparison labor — a workout with no artefact — within the same week,
and write both notes side by side. If the two tirednesses feel identical to
you, say so plainly; a genuine null result is still a result, and unit 1 ends
on whichever answer you actually got, not the one the syllabus expects.
