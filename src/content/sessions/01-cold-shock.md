This is the practicum the whole course rehearses in miniature: induce
something, notice it while it is happening, and only then try to describe it.
Do the three steps in that order. Writing while you are still cold turns the
exercise into commentary; writing a day later turns it into memory. Neither is
what week 1 is for.

## Before the practicum

Pick a cold-water induction you can safely repeat: a cold shower turned to its
coldest setting, an open-water swim, an ice bath if you have access to one.
Nothing here requires suffering — thirty seconds of genuinely cold water is
enough. If cold water is medically inadvisable for you, an ice pack held
against bare skin for sixty seconds produces the same shockwave; use that
instead and say so in your log.

## In the practicum

Enter the cold with no plan for what you'll write. Let the gasp happen. Stay in
long enough that the shock resolves into something else — usually somewhere
between fifteen seconds and two minutes. The moment it stops being new is the
thing to notice: not when the water stops feeling cold, but when your
attention stops being occupied by the fact of it.

## Afterwards

Write the note in two parts, in order: what your body did before you had a
word for it, and what you told yourself was happening once language caught up.
Most people's first drafts collapse the two. The gap between them is the
subject of this course, and this is the smallest, cleanest place to see it.
