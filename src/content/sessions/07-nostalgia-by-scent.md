Smell reaches memory by a shorter route than any other sense, bypassing the
relay station the others pass through on their way to it — which is why an
old smell can deliver a whole scene before you've consciously placed what
you're smelling. This week uses that shortcut deliberately.

## Before the practicum

Do not pre-select a smell you already know is loaded for you — that turns the
exercise into recall, not discovery. Instead, go somewhere with many old,
uncurated smells: a secondhand bookshop, a grandparent's cupboard, a school
hall, an attic. Let the smell find you.

## In the practicum

When something pulls, stop and let the memory arrive in full before you start
describing it in words. Note how much time passed between the smell and the
memory — for most people it's close to instant, which is itself worth
recording.

## Afterwards

Write the memory down in as much sensory detail as you can recover, then
answer honestly: could you have retrieved this memory on command, without the
smell, if someone had simply asked you to? For most people the answer is no.
That gap — a whole scene, unavailable to deliberate recall but one breath away
from an old smell — is this week's material.
