## The brief

Curate two experiences this course is naming for the first time here, not one
of the twelve weekly practicums: the tight, buzzing thrill of anticipation
right before something begins — a concert's first note, a rollercoaster's
drop, stepping off a plane into an unfamiliar country — and the slow,
radiating comfort of early morning sunlight on skin that was cold moments
before.

Curate each one twice: once with someone you already know, and once with
someone you don't. "Someone" doesn't have to mean exactly one person on
either side — a known group and a single stranger, a close friend and an
unfamiliar crowd, whatever actually fits the experience you're staging — so
long as you say explicitly which side is which.

The room this brief leaves is how you stage each of the four attempts. The
judgement it tests is whether you can actually locate what the company
changed, not just assert that it did.

## What you submit

Four short dated accounts, one per curated attempt (anticipation with a known
companion, anticipation with a stranger, sunlight with a known companion,
sunlight with a stranger), submitted together as a single document. For each,
name who was there and describe, in sensory and temporal detail, what
specifically differed because of it.
