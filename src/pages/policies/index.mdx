# Policies and support

## Academic integrity, for a course about introspection

Every assessment in this course asks for your own description of your own
experience. That's a different integrity risk than a normal essay: it's much
harder to plagiarise a sensation, but much easier to fabricate one. Submitting
a description of an experience you did not actually attempt to have — inventing
the "aha!" moment you never noticed, or the ache you never let yourself feel —
is treated as an integrity breach on the same footing as copied work, because
it counterfeits the one thing this course actually measures.

If a practicum genuinely didn't produce anything noticeable, say so. An honest
"I tried the induction and nothing happened" is worth marks. A confident
description of an experience you skipped is not.

## Late work and extensions

Assessments are due at the time stated on each assessment's page. There is no
late-submission window: work submitted after the deadline is not accepted.
Extensions are available, but only if requested and agreed **before** the
deadline passes — get in touch with the teaching team as early as you know
you'll need one, not after the fact.

## The experience grant

A $100 grant is disbursed on alternate weeks — weeks 1, 3, 5, 7, 9 and 11 —
toward that week's practicum. You don't have to spend it the week it lands,
and you don't have to spend it on that week's experience: if cold water is
genuinely out of reach in week 1 but a dark-sky trip is within reach that
same month, carry the money forward and spend it there instead. What you
can't do is bank it past the semester or spend it on anything outside the
twelve experiences this course names — every dollar disbursed has to end up
funding an attempt at one of them, and by week 12 all of it needs to be
accounted for.

Every dollar spent needs a dated receipt attached to the
[noticing log](/assessments/noticing-log/) entry it funded. This is the same
evidentiary logic as the log itself: a receipt dated the day of the
induction is worth more than a description of one, for the same reason a
same-day entry is worth more than one written a week later.

This exists to make an honest thing visible: money changes what's available
to you as "the same" experience. A cold shower and a cryotherapy chamber are
not obviously identical inductions, and neither are a backyard and a
dark-sky reserve an hour's drive away. Notice what the money changed about
the experience, and say so in the log — don't launder it into the account as
if it weren't a factor.

## Practicum participation

The twelve practicums ask you to actually attempt an experience, not just
read about one, and participation is mandatory. The
[noticing log](/assessments/noticing-log/) already sets the floor: a dated
entry for at least 6 of weeks 1–10's ten practicums. There is no course-wide
option to opt out of practicums altogether — the one carve-out is week 11's,
below, and it exists for a narrower, wellbeing-specific reason than simply
preferring not to.

## Grief week — support and opt-out

Week 11's practicum asks you to sit with an experience of grief that is
already part of your life; it does not ask anyone to manufacture a new loss.
That said, this is the one week in the course where the target experience can
carry real weight, and this policy exists so no one has to navigate that
weight silently.

If any part of week 11 doesn't feel like the right thing to do right now, you
have three options, all fully legitimate and none of them penalised:

- **participate as designed** — sit with the practicum as written
- **submit an alternative reflection** — write instead about how you'd expect
  to approach the practicum, without doing it, and why you're choosing not to
- **opt out entirely** — skip the week's practicum outright; it does not
  affect your standing in the [noticing log](/assessments/noticing-log/) or
  any other assessment

Reach out to [Sef Amaral](/people/sef-amaral/), the course tutor, before or
during week 11 if you want to talk through which option makes sense for you,
or if anything in that week brings up something you need support with beyond
the course itself.
